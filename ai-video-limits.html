Five common problems with AI video

AI video can save a shoot, a location and a lot of time. It also fails in predictable ways. Here is what goes wrong, what we do about it, and when filming for real is the better buy.

  • 1 Consistency
  • 2 Text, logos and detail
  • 3 Precise control
  • 4 Rights and disclosure
  • 5 Real people and trust
  1. 1

    Things change between shots

    A face, an outfit, a product or a room can look slightly different from one clip to the next. In a single shot nobody notices. Across a 60-second spot, it reads as a different person or a different product.

    How we handle it

    We lock reference images for every character and product before generating, keep scenes short, and fix drift in the edit and grade.

    Shoot instead when

    The same person or product has to appear on screen for most of the video.

  2. 2

    Text, logos and fine detail break

    Generated video still garbles lettering, packaging, screens, hands and small product details. A logo that is almost right is worse than no logo.

    How we handle it

    We never let AI draw your brand. Logos, text, UI and packaging are added afterwards as real graphics, so they are exact in every frame.

    Shoot instead when

    The video is about how a physical product looks, works or is held.

  3. 3

    Exact direction is hard to get

    A specific camera move, a precise action or timing that lands on a beat can take many attempts, and some shots never come out right. Clips are also short, so longer moments have to be built from pieces.

    How we handle it

    We storyboard first, agree which shots must be exact, and plan the edit around what AI does well. Revision rounds are agreed up front.

    Shoot instead when

    You need a choreographed action, a demonstration or a long unbroken take.

  4. 4

    Rights and disclosure are still unsettled

    Copyright in AI output is not as clear as it is for filmed footage. Many platforms ask you to label realistic AI content, and some broadcasters, clients and industries restrict it.

    How we handle it

    We use commercially licensed tools, never imitate a real person, brand or artist, and tell you up front where labels or restrictions apply.

    Shoot instead when

    You need clear ownership of every frame, or the video is going to broadcast or a regulated industry.

  5. 5

    Real people and real trust

    Generated faces and voices can look and sound slightly off, especially when speaking with emotion. When the point of a video is that people are real, like a testimonial or a founder's story, audiences notice and trust drops.

    How we handle it

    We keep generated people for background, illustration and concept work, and never present AI people as real customers or staff.

    Shoot instead when

    The video depends on a real person: testimonials, interviews, leadership messages and recruitment.

AI or a traditional shoot?

A quick way to decide. Many of our projects end up being a mix of both.

AI video

Choose AI when

  • The visuals are hard or costly to film: other places, other eras, the impossible
  • You need b-roll, concept visuals or mood pieces
  • Speed and budget matter more than exact detail
Traditional shoot

Shoot when

  • Real people, real places or a real product are the point
  • Every detail and every frame has to be exact
  • You need clear rights for broadcast or a regulated industry
Both

Mix them when

  • Filmed people and product, with generated scenes around them
  • A shoot day covers the essentials and AI fills the gaps
  • You want more versions and cut-downs than one shoot provides
